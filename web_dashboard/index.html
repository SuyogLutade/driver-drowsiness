MATHEMATICAL FORMULATION • MODULE 1

Gradient Magnitude & Eye Density Analysis

E = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{(x,y) \in \text{EyeROI}} \sqrt{G_x(x,y)^2 + G_y(x,y)^2}

Where Gx and Gy are directional spatial image gradients computed via central differences.

👀 Eyes Wide Open (High Entropy)

Eyelashes, pupil boundaries, iris rings, and sclera contours produce strong gradient vectors. Edge density E remains above 90–100% of baseline.

😴 Eyes Closed (Low Entropy)

Smooth eyelid skin lacks sharp contours. Edge magnitude collapses by >35% (E < 0.65 × Baseline). If sustained for ≥ 7 frames (~0.7s), audio alarm sounds.

COLOR SPACE SEGMENTATION • MODULE 2

YCbCr Skin Segmentation & Head Pose Drop

C_b = 0.148 \cdot R - 0.291 \cdot G + 0.439 \cdot B + 128 \\ C_r = 0.439 \cdot R - 0.368 \cdot G - 0.071 \cdot B + 128

Skin is classified if: 130 < Cr < 180 AND 80 < Cb < 160

Why YCbCr over RGB?

RGB channels couple chrominance (color) and luminance (light). YCbCr separates luminance (Y) from chrominance (Cb, Cr), ensuring robust skin classification under shadows and varying cabin lighting.

Trigger Condition

When driver droops head forward, visible facial skin area drops by > 10%. If sustained across 10 consecutive frames (~0.5s), emergency alarm is triggered.

EXAMINER DEFENSE PREPARATION

Top Viva Questions & Technical Answers

Q1: Why use gradient edge density rather than deep learning or CNNs?
Edge-density runs in < 5 ms without requiring an expensive GPU or heavy neural weights. It runs smoothly on embedded vehicle microprocessors (Raspberry Pi, low-cost ECU chips) and never suffers from neural network hallucinations.
Q2: How does the system handle drivers wearing spectacles or glasses?
Eyeglass frames produce fixed, static edge gradients that are included during the 5-frame baseline calibration. When eyes close, only the eyelid area drops in gradient magnitude, while frame edges stay constant, preventing false positives.
Q3: How do you prevent false alarms when the driver just blinks naturally?
A natural human blink lasts only 100–300 ms (1 to 3 frames at 30 FPS). Our consecutive counter requires 7 continuous closed frames (~0.7 sec). Any open frame instantly resets the counter to zero.
